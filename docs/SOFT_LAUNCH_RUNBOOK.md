# GrantForgeUSA Founding-Pilot Runbook

**Launch mode:** controlled public preview  
**Cohort limit:** 3–5 accepted organizations  
**Payments on public site:** disabled  
**Automated public matching:** disabled  
**Search indexing:** disabled

## 1. Application screening

For each pilot application:

1. Confirm that the requester represents an identifiable organization or classroom project.
2. Confirm that the project, people served, approximate amount, and desired timing are sufficiently defined.
3. Reject or request clarification when the application contains sensitive personal data, unsupported claims, prohibited activity, or materially incomplete information.
4. Record the application date and screening decision in a private operating log.
5. Do not promise acceptance, eligibility, funding, or a specific delivery time before verification.

## 2. Official opportunity verification

Before accepting an engagement:

1. Locate the opportunity through an official federal source.
2. Capture the opportunity number, title, agency, current notice URL, current status, close date, award range, cost-share requirement, and eligible applicant categories.
3. Read the current notice and every posted amendment. Do not rely on a search-result summary alone.
4. Confirm that the requested amount is within the published range or document the reason for a different approach.
5. Identify required registrations, attachments, certifications, page limits, evaluation criteria, and submission method.
6. Record the verification date and official source URL.
7. Decline the engagement when basic applicant eligibility or deadline feasibility cannot be supported.

## 3. Written acceptance and scope

Send written acceptance only after verification. The acceptance message must state:

- the selected official opportunity;
- the confirmed pricing tier;
- included deliverables;
- information still required from the applicant;
- delivery target and timing assumptions;
- that the deliverable is an editable draft package;
- that the applicant performs final review, certification, and submission;
- that funding and agency acceptance are not guaranteed.

No charge should be requested through the soft-launch website. Any pilot payment method must be separately verified and documented before use.

## 4. Draft production

1. Create a structured requirements matrix from the official notice.
2. Separate verified applicant facts from assumptions, placeholders, and suggested language.
3. Draft only from information the applicant supplied or can substantiate.
4. Never invent outcomes, partners, budgets, statistics, registrations, certifications, or organizational history.
5. Mark all missing facts and applicant decisions clearly.
6. Keep the official opportunity URL and verification date with the working file.
7. Treat software-generated language as draft material, not authority.

## 5. Human quality review

Before delivery, verify:

- organization name and applicant type;
- opportunity number, agency, deadline, award range, and cost-share requirement;
- alignment with eligibility and published priorities;
- consistency among narrative, activities, outcomes, timeline, and budget language;
- absence of invented facts or unsupported claims;
- clear placeholders for missing attachments or decisions;
- plain disclosure that the applicant must review and submit;
- accessible, readable PDF or editable-document output.

A second pass should compare the package directly against the requirements matrix.

## 6. Delivery and revision control

1. Deliver the draft package through an approved private channel.
2. Include the official notice link and a concise applicant-action checklist.
3. State the version and delivery date.
4. Ask the applicant to confirm factual accuracy before submission use.
5. Track revisions and issue a new version number for each material update.
6. Do not retain sensitive source material longer than operationally necessary.

## 7. Pilot measurements

Track at minimum:

- applications received and accepted;
- reason for each decline;
- verification time;
- drafting time;
- total turnaround time;
- number and type of applicant corrections;
- missed or ambiguous notice requirements found during review;
- revision count;
- applicant satisfaction and usefulness feedback;
- support incidents, payment issues, or privacy concerns.

## 8. Stop conditions

Pause new applications immediately if any of the following occurs:

- an unverified or closed opportunity is presented as current;
- the site or workflow exposes applicant data;
- payment status, order state, or delivery access cannot be reconstructed reliably;
- a material legal, privacy, security, or merchant-account issue is discovered;
- pilot volume exceeds human-review capacity;
- drafts repeatedly contain invented facts, unsupported certifications, or missed requirements.

Resume only after the cause is documented, corrected, and retested.
